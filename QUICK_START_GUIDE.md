Welcome! This guide walks through building your first character and playing it.

# Building a Character

Start in **Character Mode** (top right). Everything you set here is saved to your character sheet and carries over into Play Mode automatically. The left sidebar is grouped into **Character** (Traits & Attributes), **Abilities** (Education, Spell Book, Techniques), **Equipment** (Shop, Loadout, Armor), and **Summary** (Playsheet). Saving, loading, and account actions live in the **menu (☰)** at the top right.

## 1. Name, Species & Traits

On the Traits & Attributes page: character name, Species, Sex, Age, Height, Build, Social and Mental Disposition, Native Language, and **Class** (Warrior, Disciple, or Caster). Species and Body Type drive a lot of the math later (size, speed, natural attributes), so pick those first. **Class** sets how many techniques you earn per level (see Techniques below). Everything else is flavor you can freely change.

## 2. Raw Stats

You have **80 points** to spend across seven Raw Stats: Strength, Endurance, Agility, Memory, Intellect, Senses, and Quintessence. Use the +/- buttons or type a value directly. The "Points Used" bar tracks how much of your 80 you've spent — go over and it turns red, but nothing stops you from adjusting until it's balanced.

Quintessence is your magical aptitude — leave it at 0 for a non-caster, or invest in it if you plan to cast spells.

## 3. Final Attributes

These are computed automatically from your Raw Stats plus your Species, Age, Build, and Disposition choices — Power, Speed, Toughness, Stamina, Manual Dexterity, Physical Coordination, Knowledge, Social Skills, Logic, Intuition, and the Senses. You don't spend points here directly; they update live as you change the raw stats above them.

## 4. Education

Education is bought with a separate pool of Education Points (shown on the Education page), spent on the subjects you want to study. Your success chance in a subject depends on the points invested and the Final Attributes it's tied to — with diminishing returns: the first point is worth the most, the second half as much, the third a quarter, and so on.

## 5. Shop & Loadout — Weapons & Equipment

You start with a **Purse** of money. Use the **Shop** to buy weapons, armor, and other gear (or **Acquire** something you find, which is noted in the Journal for the GM). The **Loadout** page then shows where your things are: what you are **carrying** (it counts toward your weight, and a weapon you carry can be held in your right or left hand during play), what is **in storage** (kept safe, weighs nothing, can't be held), and what you have **given away**. Use **Store**/**Take** to move things between carrying and storage, and **Give…** to hand an item to another character, a mount, or a monster that is on the active map. Some weapons can also be thrown (they leave your hand and land near the target).

## 6. Armor

Equip the armor pieces you own into each body slot (Head, Torso, Arms, Legs, etc. — the exact slots depend on your Body Type). Armor reduces incoming damage by type (slash/pierce/crush) but can carry an encumbrance cost, and some pieces have an **Arcane Impedance** that lowers your maximum spell success (shown on the Playsheet).

## 7. Spell Book (casters only)

At least 1 point in Quintessence makes you eligible to permanently memorize any spell — magic-related education (Arcane, Deific, Demonic, Nature) isn't required to learn one, it only boosts your casting success later. Memorizing spends Memory Points (Knowledge × Level, shown at the top of the page) — a spell you're a poor match for costs extra to hold onto. Once memorized, a spell is yours for good. Casting itself happens in Play Mode, only from what you've memorized here; a spell's range and your own senses affect how easy it is to hit with.

## 8. Techniques

Techniques are fighting skills tied to a weapon type (Axe, Longsword, Bow, and so on). Each weapon type has **Technique I, II, and III**, unlocking at levels 1, 2, and 3, plus a **Parry** technique. You choose techniques from the Techniques page, up to your class's allowance per level × your Level (the GM sets the allowance — by default Warriors get 3 per level, Disciples 2, Casters 1). A technique only applies while you use a weapon of that type: it adds to your hit chance and damage (or to your Parry), and it's called out in the hit roll and damage readout so you can see it counted. You can respec (clear and re-choose) freely while the game is in development.

## 9. Save

Open the **menu (☰)** at the top right and choose **Save Character** — it writes everything to your account (the sidebar shows when it last saved). Reload it any time with **Load Character**, and use **Select New Campaign/Character** or **Join a Campaign** to switch games. Once you join your GM's campaign, your character shows up for them to add to the map.

# Leveling Up

Your character's Level is derived automatically from XP — the GM awards XP after combat (via "Finish Combat") or with **Grant XP**, and your Level updates on its own. **Each level above 1 grants +1 to every Raw Stat**, shown on the Raw Stats panel as a small badge next to each stat. Leveling also unlocks higher-tier techniques and gives you more technique slots and Memory Points. You never spend points on the stat bonus — it's automatic.

# Playing in Play Mode

Play Mode is where your character moves around the map, fights, and casts spells alongside everyone else at the table.

- **Left panel:** your portrait, level, class, Health, Conditions, and an **Initiative** roll button when combat starts. **Abilities** has **View** buttons for your education (click a percentage to roll it), spell book, and techniques; **Equipment** lets you change what's in each hand; **View All** shows each held weapon's hit chance and damage. The ✎ button jumps back to Character Mode.
- **On your turn:** open **Actions** to choose moves, attacks, spells, and defensive stances such as Sentinel (react to anyone who steps into melee range), Target (the same for ranged), and Vigilant (watch for movers in a cone). Your hit chance depends on the weapon, your target's size, range, and lighting; a target in darkness is harder to hit at range.
- **Seeing the map:** you only see what your character can see — walls block sight, dark areas need light (torches, campfires, light spells) or Night Vision, and places you've explored stay dimly remembered.
- **Journal:** the Journal (bottom left) logs everything that happens, and is saved for the campaign, so you can scroll back if you missed something.
- **Menu (☰):** top right — switch campaign, join one, edit your profile, report a bug, or sign out.
