# Play Mode

Once you have designed and equipped your character, you are ready for Play Mode. Play Mode is where your character moves around the map, fights, and casts spells alongside everyone else at the table. From Character Mode, click **Play Mode** in the upper **right** corner. To go back, click **Character Mode** in the same spot. The **menu (☰)** next to it lets you switch campaign, join one, edit your profile, report a bug, or sign out.

## Character Panel

The left panel shows the character selected on the map — at first, yours. During combat it shows whoever's turn it is. At the top are the portrait, name, level, class, and tiles for Height, Weight, Speed, Size, and Blood. The ✎ button jumps back to Character Mode to edit your character. Sections can be collapsed with the arrow on the right of each heading.

### Health

These bars show your current Toughness (hit points), Stamina (energy), Blood (life), and Quintessence (casters). Damage lowers Toughness first, then Blood.

* If your Toughness reaches 0, you are unconscious and fall prone (you may be able to revive on your own, and a healing potion will also do the trick).
* Damage beyond Toughness subtracts Blood. Your character weakens as Blood drops, and if it runs out, your character dies. Some damage causes bleeding over time.
* Stamina is spent by actions and comes back with rest. Moving more than half your movement in a round also costs 1 Stamina (the path you draw shows "−1 Stamina" when it will). If it runs out, you fall prone and have to stand up again.
* Casters also have Quintessence, their magic power. It is spent on spells and some magic items and slowly comes back (about 1 point every 3 rounds). When a spell costs more Quintessence than you have, the rest is paid from Stamina, then Toughness, then Blood — desperate times call for desperate measures.

### Conditions

This lists the conditions affecting the character. The GM and the game assign and clear them; players can't change them. They fall into groups:

* **Downed:** Unconscious, Dead.
* **Stances you chose:** Blocking, Parrying, Evading, Sentinel, Target, Vigilant, Infiltrating, Searching. A stance guards until you drop it. If the GM refreshes the page mid-session, a token that was still guarding shows its stance as active again in the action dialog (it can be dropped there).
* **Effects:** Bleeding, Stunned, Slowed, Blinded, Deafened, Paralyzed, Prone, Burning, Corroding, Regenerating, Bathed.
* **Grappling:** Held, Holding, Carrying.

### Combat

A melee critical miss (an attack roll of 96 or more always misses) can go wrong: 96-97 you may slip (fail a Physical Coordination save and you fall Prone), 98-99 you may fumble (fail a Manual Dexterity save and you drop the weapon), and on 100 you may hit yourself (fail a Physical Coordination save and you take half a damage roll, ignoring armor). Shots, throws and spells have no such table. A critical miss during a Whirlwind also breaks the spin: the remaining swings are lost.

**Preferences.** Open the menu (☰) and choose Profile: *Quick roll by default* swaps the spinning roller for a short pause before the number appears (the Quick roll box in the roller changes the same setting), and *Center the map on the active character in combat* turns the automatic slide to whoever's turn it is on or off. They are saved to your account as you tick them.

**Character Mode and the right character (GM).** During combat, the ✎ Character Mode button opens the *active combatant's* own record, so you edit the character who is actually in play. If the character loaded in Character Mode is not the one on the map (two records can share a name), a banner at the top says so and offers **Open the one on the map**.

**Seeing a monster (players).** When a monster is the active combatant, or you click its token, you see its name, its health bars (never its Quintessence), its conditions and what it is holding. Its stats, skills, armor and the rest of its sheet stay with the GM.

**Sentinel and Target reactions.** When a mover crosses into several Sentinel or Target zones, each watcher is asked in the order the mover reaches them, and a watcher's attack is rolled on the watcher's own screen (the GM for monsters). The mover waits until that attack is finished before the next watcher is asked. If a reaction knocks the mover out, they stay where they fell.

**Passing a character to another owner (GM).** In the Tokens panel, each campaign character has a **⇄** button. Choose who gets it: any player in the campaign, or you (which turns it into an NPC). The character keeps everything (XP, gear, storage, purse, token); the new owner finds it in their character list, and the old owner loses access. Only the GM can do this, so there is no export/import and no duplicate.

**Entering a roll by hand.** Every roller has a small **Enter roll** box. Tick it, type the number from a physical die (or whatever the situation calls for) and press Enter; it is treated exactly like a rolled number. It stays on until you reload the page. Saves that can be refused (area spells) also have a **Don't resist** button, which counts as a failed save, for a willing ally or a buff.

**Area spell saves.** When an area spell catches people, each caught token pulses. A player clicks their own pulsing token to roll their own save (the GM's screen applies the result); the GM clicks any other token to roll for it. The panel's **Roll Creatures Automatically** handles monsters in one go, and **Roll for Players Too** covers anyone who is away.

**Importing and exporting walls (GM).** In Manage Walls, **Export walls** saves the map's walls and doors to a file and **Import walls** loads one (it shows the count and asks whether to replace or add to the existing walls; unusable entries are skipped). Files can come from another map, from `tools/wall-tracer.html` (traces a dark-background map for you), or from an AI; `tools/WALLS_FORMAT.md` has the format and a ready-to-paste prompt. Doors are added by hand afterward.

**Deleting a map (GM).** In Maps & Notes, the 🗑 button beside a map deletes it permanently after a confirmation: its tokens, walls, hidden objects, pins and map notes go with it, and its picture is removed unless another map uses it. Deleting the active map leaves the table blank until you activate another.

**Throwables.** Besides the Arcane Grenade (explosion, hurls people away), you can carry a **Molotov Cocktail** (burn, may set targets on fire), **Bottled Lightning** (shock, may stun) and an **Acid Vial** (corrode, may leave targets Corroding). They are thrown the same way (pick a spot; a bad throw scatters); each has its own damage type, so armor protects against it accordingly, and anyone who fails the save may get the condition. The GM can make more in Game Config > Consumables: set a Blast Radius, a Blast Damage Type, and optionally a Blast Condition and its chance.

**Webs.** A Giant Spider (monster species, Insect body) spins webs with its Spinnerets (a natural weapon, reach 20 ft), and the Web spell and the Webspinning enchantment do the same. A hit with no damage wraps the target in webbing: **Webbed** means it cannot move (the GM can still nudge the token) until it breaks free. Spend your action on **Break Free**, a percentile save of 100 x your Power / (your Power + the web) per cent, 10% easier each round you stay stuck. The web is as strong as the spider's Power (times the weapon's Webs value) or the caster's Quintessence (never below 30); a second web only ever tightens the first. Fire (the Burning condition) burns it away at once. The numbers are in one place in the code (WEB).

**Ammunition.** Anything with "bow" in its name or Weapon Type (Bow, War 50 Pounds, Composite Bow...) shoots Arrows; crossbows and arbalests shoot Crossbow Bolts. Any item with "Arrow" (or "Bolt") in its name counts as ammunition, so custom ones like War Arrow work. Sealed bundles like "Arrows (×20)" count too; the first shot from one opens a bundle into single rounds. Every shot from a bow or crossbow spends one, and the count shows beside each held bow on the builder's Loadout tab (plus a "Projectile weapons carried" list), in the play Loadout, in Equipment > View All, and on the attack button (which is greyed out at zero: "Out of arrows"). Thrown weapons are their own ammunition and monsters are not tracked.

When combat starts, press **Roll** under Combat to roll your initiative. When it's your turn, the banner at the top of the panel shows **Actions** (choose what to do) and **Done**. The action dialog covers moving, attacking, defending, casting spells, and stances such as Sentinel (react to anyone who steps into melee range), Target (the same at range), and Vigilant (watch a cone for movement). Moving ends Sentinel, Evade, Target and Vigilant (Infiltrate is for sneaking, so it carries on). Standing up from Prone takes your whole move, or choose **Hop Up**: an Acrobatics roll (less carried gear weighs you down) for 2 Stamina that gets you up and keeps your movement; a miss leaves you Prone with your move spent, and the Stamina is gone either way. **Whirlwind** (a level 4 technique for Flails, Greatswords, Polearms, Scimitars and Staffs) sweeps every standing body within your weapon's reach (anyone Prone, unconscious or dead is skipped), allies and bystanders included: one attack each, 1 Stamina per target, and the damage cap of every swing drops by 1 for each target attacked. **Numb** (level 3, Whips, Staffs, Clubs): a hit on an arm or leg rolls its Fumble or Slow chance as if the weapon had done a d10 of damage, even if armor soaked the blow. **Lunge** (level 3, Knives, Short Swords, Longswords, Rapiers): a defensive move that lasts until you attack or the round ends; a Sentinel gets no reaction attack as you close in. Your attack on it uses up its Sentinel stance (it can still block or parry), and it attacks normally on its own turn. A melee attack can only target someone whose token edge is within the weapon's reach (at least 1 ft); click out of reach and you get a toast and can pick another target. A Sentinel's threat circle reaches as far past its token edge as the longest melee weapon it is actually holding (a weapon in the pack does not count; empty-handed is 1 ft), for characters and monsters alike. Characters and monsters the GM drops on the map gather around the middle of the GM's view, each in its own spot, so the number present is obvious. Shake Off Stun and Resuscitate (under Recover) each use up your whole turn, win or lose; a character who falls unconscious is no longer stunned.

**Knockback:** explosions (such as Arcane Blast) and heavy smashing blows (clubs, maces, hammers, some monster slams) can throw a creature backward, directly away from the blast or the attacker. A streak and floating text on the map show who was thrown and how far. Lighter creatures fly further, heavy or armored ones barely move, and anyone lying Prone is thrown half as far (and takes half damage from an explosion). Anything thrown into a wall or another creature takes slam damage — and so does whoever it hits. Grabbing, grabbed and carried creatures stay put. Anyone thrown gets a save to land on their feet (Power + 4 x Physical Coordination %, minus 4 per foot thrown, halved after a slam; pushes of 2 ft or less need no save); a miss leaves them Prone.

**Arcane Grenade:** a throwable explosive from the Shop. Hold it in a hand, choose **💣 Throw** in the Actions (or Explore) dialog and click where it should land (up to 40 ft). A throw roll decides whether it lands on the spot or scatters, and a wall in the way sets it off early. It bursts like a smaller Arcane Blast, and it catches the thrower too if they are inside the radius.

### Abilities

Click **View** next to **Education** to see your subjects with their success percentages — click a percentage to roll it. **Spell Book** (casters) shows the spells you have memorized, and **Techniques** shows the weapon techniques you have chosen. Spells are memorized in Character Mode; magic education isn't needed to learn a spell, it only improves your chance of casting it.

### Equipment (Held Items)

Equipment shows what is in your **right** and **left** hand. Want to use your sword? Equip it in one hand or both. Want to drink a potion? Equip it here and be ready for your turn. You can also drop an item here, but it lands on the map for anyone to pick up. In combat, changing what is in a hand on your turn (from the Equipment panel or the action dialog) gives a choice: **stow** the old item (this ends your turn) or **drop** it (instant, it lands on the map and you can use the new item right away). To pick something up (or grab an opponent), make sure the hand you need is free. **View All** shows the hit chance and damage of each held weapon action and everything you're carrying.

**Looting.** With a free hand, choose **🎒 Loot** (in the Actions dialog it sits under each arm; in Explore it is under Grab) and click a dead, unconscious or paralyzed body next to you. A list of its weapons and equipment appears; whatever you take goes into your pack and adds to the weight you carry. Out of combat you can take as much as you like. In combat, one item takes your whole round.

### Armor

This lists the armor you are wearing. To change it, go to Character Mode: buy pieces in the **Shop** (get the size that fits your character), then open **Loadout** and equip them on the body — head, torso, arms, and legs. Armor reduces damage but is heavy and can slow you down, and some pieces are noisy. Some pieces also have an Arcane Impedance that lowers a caster's maximum spell success.

### Enchanted Items

Some items carry an enchantment. A **passive** one is always on while you wear the item (armor in a body slot; a ring on each hand, a necklace and a belt under **Jewelry & Accessories** on the Character Mode Loadout tab) or, for anything else, hold it in a hand. An **activated** one (a wand, say) casts a spell on demand: with the item worn or in hand, the Actions and Explore dialogs show an **Enchanted Items** section. It works at a fixed chance no matter who uses it, and a failure can miscast. It costs Quintessence; if you're short, each missing point costs 4 Stamina instead (some items insist on Quintessence). In combat, activating an item ends your round.

### Full-body image

A character can have a larger full-body picture, set at the upper left of the Traits tab in Character Mode (click the box; click **Set token image** under it to crop the round token picture out of it; the picture is scaled down but not cropped, and a transparent background works). It is a visual hint of what a character carries, or what a monster looks like, without naming it. Click a token on the map and the picture appears in the upper left of the panel (replacing the round portrait), with the name, level and class, XP, species, Speed, Height, Weight and Size beside it; click it for a large view (click again or press Esc to close). Other players see it only while that token is fully visible to them: not when it is just a shadow in the dark or out of sight. A monster template's picture is copied onto every monster spawned from it. You and the GM always see yours.

### Monk

**Monk** is a class (pick it in the builder's Class box; 2 techniques per level) with its own techniques that no other class can choose. All of them work bare-handed. **Monk Block I–III** (levels 1–3): tier I lets an empty hand Block, a little weaker than a small shield, and every tier adds +5 to it. **Iron Fist** (level 2) unlocks the Iron Fist strike among the bare-handed attacks. **Soul Armor** (level 3): any body area with no armor on is protected by the Soul Armor type (set its numbers under Game Config → Armor); worn armor takes its place. **Tornado** (level 4) is a Whirlwind with bare hands: everyone standing within 5 ft, allies included, 1 Stamina each. A Monk casts Nature magic by learning Nature skills, like any other caster. Switching away from Monk drops the Monk techniques.

### Stats

Your Final Attributes (Power, Toughness, Speed, and so on). A green or red number in brackets means the stat is currently raised or lowered, for example by blood loss or a potion.

## Seeing the Map

You only see what your character can see. Walls block sight, dark areas need light (torches, campfires, light spells) or Night Vision, and places you have already explored stay dimly remembered. A foe standing in darkness is harder to hit from range. Night Vision lets you identify others in the dark out to as many feet as your Night Vision stat; a bit beyond that you only see shadowy shapes. Night Vision also shows the map itself, at 5 ft of map per point (Vigilant cones keep their own scale).

The GM may also have hidden things on the map — traps, secret doors, switches. As your character gets near one, they may notice it using sight, hearing or smell. Hidden magic (an enchantment, an illusion over a secret door, a spell on the floor) is noticed by casters through their Quintessence score, which works like sight: it needs a clear line, and the score is both the reach and the chance, with the same two rolls and the same doubling when you Search; a character with no Quintessence cannot notice it. Each sense gets two rolls per approach: one at half chance when you come within twice your sense score in feet (Vision 10 = 20 ft), and one at full chance within once your score. The better the sense and the worse it is hidden, the better the odds; walk away and back and you roll again. Reset Fog (GM) also hides everything the players had found again. If you walk onto a hidden line or area before noticing it, your move stops right there, the trap is revealed to everyone, and a dialog asks "How will [your character] respond?" preceded by a short cue if the GM gave the trap one (for example "Choppy hears a sharp metallic click."; the GM picks it in the Hidden line/area dialog from 32 sights, sounds and feelings), or "There is a suspicious click." if not, with a 6-second clock: Freeze (the default if you do not answer), Jump Back, Roll to the Side, Hunker behind Shield (only with a shield in hand), Dash Forward, Go Prone, or Other (type what you do, which stops the clock). The choice is only recorded; the GM decides what it does to the trap's effects. Every other party member gets the same call (with a note saying who set it off), so someone nearby can react; someone far away can simply let the clock run out. The GM gets a trap dialog too, which shows how the character responded; to call for a roll there, pick any skill or final stat (stat x 5 as a percentage), a difficulty and which characters should roll (the one who triggered it is ticked), and press Call for roll. When every roll is in, Apply Effects opens with the characters who failed already ticked and a Character responses box listing what each one chose. Click **Search** in the Conditions section to look carefully and double your chances; it ends when you take another action. When someone notices something it shows on everyone's map and in the Journal.

## Journal

The Journal (bottom left) records what happens during the session — attacks, damage, spells, and so on — so you can scroll back if you missed something. It is saved for the campaign, so it is still there after a reload or the next session. Only the GM can permanently clear the history (the ✕ button, with a confirmation); for players, ✕ just hides the entries on their own screen until they reload.

**Pinging the map.** To point something out to the whole table (a suspected hidden object, a route, a threat), press and hold the left mouse button on an empty spot of the map for about half a second without moving. A pulsing gold marker with your name appears for everyone, GM included, for a few seconds. Moving the mouse or releasing early just pans the map as usual. Pings don't trigger while a wall, measure, attack or pin tool is active.

**Exploring (out of combat).** When no combat is running, the left panel shows an **Explore** button. It opens a dialog like the combat Actions dialog, but nothing is timed and nothing costs stamina: pick a **stance** (Infiltrate, Search, Vigilant, Sentinel; it stays until you change it), **cast** a known spell from the same spell picker, or **swap what's in your hands** for free. When combat starts, Explore gives way to the normal Actions button.

**Skill checks.** The Explore dialog has a **Checks** section: quick buttons for *Notice* (Perception) and *Avoid a trap* (Dodge), plus a skill list. Skills you've put points into roll at your normal chance. A set of everyday skills (Climbing, Swimming, Tracking, Sneaking, Navigation, Investigation, First Aid and similar) can also be tried **untrained at half the odds of a first point**; languages, literacy, history and the like need points. The GM sets the **difficulty** at the top of the section: **Easy** doubles everyone's chance, **Normal** leaves it, **Difficult** halves it, and players see the current setting. Results go in the Journal.

**Resting.** The Explore dialog has a Rest section. *Catch your breath* restores all of a character's Stamina, free and untimed, any time you're out of combat. Only the GM can have the party *Recuperate*: the GM sets how many days of rest are available, and each day heals 1 Toughness and 1 Blood (plus full Stamina and Quintessence), for one character or the whole party.

**Calling for a roll (GM).** In a character's Explore dialog, pick a skill in the Checks list, set the difficulty, and press *Call*. Every player gets a prompt to roll that check for their own character, and the results land in the Journal.

**Apply Effects (GM).** In the Actions tab, *⚡ Apply Effects* opens a dialog for hitting several tokens at once, in or out of combat. Tick the targets (quick buttons for Party, Monsters, All), then tick any mix of: a **condition** (add or remove, e.g. Prone), a **stat change** (e.g. Hearing −3, lasting until you clear the Buffed condition), and **damage** (a type, a min–max range rolled separately for each target, with an option to ignore armor). Results go to the Journal, and anyone brought to 0 Toughness or Blood goes down as usual.

**Dropping items (GM).** The *📦 Items* tool in the Map Setup bar works like Lights: pick a weapon, piece of equipment or consumable from the list and click the map to drop it there (Esc stops). It becomes an ordinary ground item that characters can pick up with a free hand, loot or carry.

**Doors (GM).** In wall-drawing mode, pick *🚪 Door* from the shape list and click on an existing Line or Rect wall. The wall splits and a 5-foot door takes the gap. Doors are drawn for the GM everywhere and for players only in areas they have seen (so Reset Fog hides them again);  solid brown when closed and a faint dotted line when open. A closed door blocks sight like the wall; an open one lets sight through. Double-click a door on the map (outside any drawing mode) to open or close it, or use the Open/Close button in *Manage* walls. **Secret doors (GM):** pick *🚪🕵 Secret door* and click a wall the same way, then name it and set how well it is hidden from sight, hearing and smell (like a hidden line). It has three states: **hidden** (plain wall to the players; the GM sees a purple marker), **noticed** (a character found it, so it now shows as a closed door), and **open**. Characters notice it the same way they notice any hidden object, and it never springs anything. Double-click it to step hidden, noticed, open, then back to noticed, or pick the state in *Manage* walls. Reset Fog hides it again and shuts it.

**Sitting out a combat (GM).** Each row in the combat order has a **💤 ZZZ** button. Pressing it marks that combatant inactive: the turn order skips them, they are never asked for (or auto-rolled) initiative, and they get no end-of-round upkeep, while their token stays on the map. Players don't see inactive combatants in the order. Press ZZZ again to bring them back in.

**Starting combat (GM).** Pressing **⚔ Start** rolls initiative for everyone in the order who hasn't rolled yet and isn't ZZZ'd, so there is no separate "Roll Remaining" button. Anyone who already rolled keeps their number, so let players roll first if you want them to do it by hand.

**Walls stop movement, and movement has gaits.** Walls drawn with the 🚶 flag now block tokens: a move stops just short of the first wall, a closed door is a wall, and an open door is not. (Outside combat the GM can move any token anywhere, walls included; in combat the GM can hold Shift to pass through walls.) In combat, how far you move in a turn is measured in seconds of your Speed: up to 1 second is a **Walk** (free), 2 seconds a **Jog** (1 Stamina), 3 seconds a **Run** (2 Stamina), and 4 seconds a **Sprint** (3 Stamina). Four seconds is the most you can move in a turn, so a route stops there. While you plan a move, the path changes colour with the gait, small markers show where each gait begins and its cost, and the label shows the Stamina you will pay. A red X marks where a wall stops you and a ring marks the sprint limit.

The same gaits apply when the GM moves a monster on its turn: dragging the active monster, or routing it with Path, is limited to four seconds and costs the monster Stamina.

When the GM applies an effect to your character, a dialog pops up on your screen listing what happened (a condition, a stat change, damage), so you don't miss it. Press OK to dismiss it.
