# Play Mode

Once you have designed and equipped your character, you are ready for Play Mode. Play Mode is where your character moves around the map, fights, and casts spells alongside everyone else at the table. From Character Mode, click **Play Mode** in the upper **right** corner. To go back, click **Character Mode** in the same spot. The **menu (☰)** next to it lets you switch campaign, join one, edit your profile, report a bug, or sign out.

## Character Panel

The left panel shows the character selected on the map — at first, yours. During combat it shows whoever's turn it is. At the top are the portrait, name, level, class, and tiles for Height, Weight, Speed, Size, and Blood. The ✎ button jumps back to Character Mode to edit your character. Sections can be collapsed with the arrow on the right of each heading.

### Health

These bars show your current Toughness (hit points), Stamina (energy), Blood (life), and Quintessence (casters). Damage lowers Toughness first, then Blood.

* If your Toughness reaches 0, you are unconscious and fall prone (you may be able to revive on your own, and a healing potion will also do the trick).
* Damage beyond Toughness subtracts Blood. Your character weakens as Blood drops, and if it runs out, your character dies. Some damage causes bleeding over time.
* Stamina is spent by actions and comes back with rest. Moving more than half your movement in a round also costs 1 Stamina (the path you draw shows "−1 Stamina" when it will). If it runs out, you fall prone and have to stand up again.
* Casters also have Quintessence, their magic power. It is spent on spells and some magic items and slowly comes back (about 1 point every 3 rounds). When a spell costs more Quintessence than you have, the rest is paid from Stamina, then Toughness, then Blood — desperate times call for desperate measures.

### Conditions

This lists the conditions affecting the character. The GM and the game assign and clear them; players can't change them. They fall into groups:

* **Downed:** Unconscious, Dead.
* **Stances you chose:** Blocking, Parrying, Evading, Sentinel, Target, Vigilant, Infiltrating, Searching.
* **Effects:** Bleeding, Stunned, Slowed, Blinded, Deafened, Paralyzed, Prone, Burning, Corroding, Regenerating, Bathed.
* **Grappling:** Held, Holding, Carrying.

### Combat

When combat starts, press **Roll** under Combat to roll your initiative. When it's your turn, the banner at the top of the panel shows **Actions** (choose what to do) and **Done**. The action dialog covers moving, attacking, defending, casting spells, and stances such as Sentinel (react to anyone who steps into melee range), Target (the same at range), and Vigilant (watch a cone for movement). Moving ends Sentinel, Evade, Target and Vigilant (Infiltrate is for sneaking, so it carries on). Shake Off Stun and Resuscitate (under Recover) each use up your whole turn, win or lose; a character who falls unconscious is no longer stunned.

### Abilities

Click **View** next to **Education** to see your subjects with their success percentages — click a percentage to roll it. **Spell Book** (casters) shows the spells you have memorized, and **Techniques** shows the weapon techniques you have chosen. Spells are memorized in Character Mode; magic education isn't needed to learn a spell, it only improves your chance of casting it.

### Equipment (Held Items)

Equipment shows what is in your **right** and **left** hand. Want to use your sword? Equip it in one hand or both. Want to drink a potion? Equip it here and be ready for your turn. You can also drop an item here, but it lands on the map for anyone to pick up. In combat, changing what is in a hand on your turn (from the Equipment panel or the action dialog) gives a choice: **stow** the old item (this ends your turn) or **drop** it (instant, it lands on the map and you can use the new item right away). To pick something up (or grab an opponent), make sure the hand you need is free. **View All** shows the hit chance and damage of each held weapon action and everything you're carrying.

### Armor

This lists the armor you are wearing. To change it, go to Character Mode: buy pieces in the **Shop** (get the size that fits your character), then open **Armor** and equip them on the body — head, torso, arms, and legs. Armor reduces damage but is heavy and can slow you down, and some pieces are noisy. Some pieces also have an Arcane Impedance that lowers a caster's maximum spell success.

### Stats

Your Final Attributes (Power, Toughness, Speed, and so on). A green or red number in brackets means the stat is currently raised or lowered, for example by blood loss or a potion.

## Seeing the Map

You only see what your character can see. Walls block sight, dark areas need light (torches, campfires, light spells) or Night Vision, and places you have already explored stay dimly remembered. A foe standing in darkness is harder to hit from range.

The GM may also have hidden things on the map — traps, secret doors, switches. As your character passes near one, they may notice it using sight, hearing or smell (the better your senses and the worse it is hidden, the better the odds). Click **Search** in the Conditions section to look carefully and double your chances; it ends when you take another action. When someone notices something it shows on everyone's map and in the Journal.

## Journal

The Journal (bottom left) records what happens during the session — attacks, damage, spells, and so on — so you can scroll back if you missed something. It is saved for the campaign, so it is still there after a reload or the next session. Only the GM can permanently clear the history (the ✕ button, with a confirmation); for players, ✕ just hides the entries on their own screen until they reload.

**Pinging the map.** To point something out to the whole table (a suspected hidden object, a route, a threat), press and hold the left mouse button on an empty spot of the map for about half a second without moving. A pulsing gold marker with your name appears for everyone, GM included, for a few seconds. Moving the mouse or releasing early just pans the map as usual. Pings don't trigger while a wall, measure, attack or pin tool is active.

**Exploring (out of combat).** When no combat is running, the left panel shows an **Explore** button. It opens a dialog like the combat Actions dialog, but nothing is timed and nothing costs stamina: pick a **stance** (Infiltrate, Search, Vigilant, Sentinel; it stays until you change it), **cast** a known spell from the same spell picker, or **swap what's in your hands** for free. When combat starts, Explore gives way to the normal Actions button.

**Skill checks.** The Explore dialog has a **Checks** section: quick buttons for *Notice* (Perception) and *Avoid a trap* (Dodge), plus a skill list. Skills you've put points into roll at your normal chance. A set of everyday skills (Climbing, Swimming, Tracking, Sneaking, Navigation, Investigation, First Aid and similar) can also be tried **untrained at half the odds of a first point**; languages, literacy, history and the like need points. The GM sets the **difficulty** at the top of the section: **Easy** doubles everyone's chance, **Normal** leaves it, **Difficult** halves it, and players see the current setting. Results go in the Journal.

**Resting.** The Explore dialog has a Rest section. *Catch your breath* restores all of a character's Stamina, free and untimed, any time you're out of combat. Only the GM can call an *Extended rest*, for one character or the whole party; it restores Stamina and Quintessence (wounds and Blood are not healed).

**Calling for a roll (GM).** In a character's Explore dialog, pick a skill in the Checks list, set the difficulty, and press *Call*. Every player gets a prompt to roll that check for their own character, and the results land in the Journal.
